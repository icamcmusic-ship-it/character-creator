How to use, and how generation works

The 30-second version. Set a few sliders (or none), press Build & Roll, and read the sheet. Every card is one trait; toss any card you don't like, keep the ones you love, then build again — kept cards survive. That loop (generate → lock the keepers → regenerate the rest) is the intended way to work, not getting a perfect sheet on roll one.

Sliders. The centre of each personality slider is a real neutral band, not a hair-trigger between two poles. Below about 14 you get genuinely balanced or situational traits — "warm one-to-one, flat in groups," "honest about facts, evasive about feelings" — and ±5 draws from the same middle pool, so small settings either side of zero aren't different people. From roughly 14 to 42 the middle fades gradually into the leaning side; past that you're committed. Direction picks which side; distance from centre picks intensity, on an eased curve that keeps the bottom of the dial quiet. Honesty at −20 gives small, deniable evasions; −90 gives someone who lies about the weather. The three voice sliders (Verbosity / Register / Composure) don't just shape speech — they also pull the deep sections: high volatility drags Attachment toward Disorganized, high verbosity favours the Connector role, and so on.

Per-card controls. lock = keep this exact trait. pin = keep this intensity level while letting the trait vary (use +/− to nudge it). reroll = redraw, skipping what you already rejected here (until the pool runs out, when it says so rather than silently doing nothing). why? = exactly which sliders and resolved traits caused this pick — the generator never hides its reasoning.

Deeper tools. Archetypes blend a preset posture with your sliders (they nudge, never overwrite). Depth-first flips the whole flow: roll the wound/values/attachment first and derive personality from them. Seed from a trait builds a character around one trait you've picked by hand. Under pressure adds a second sheet showing how the voice degrades under stress. The Cast and Relationships tabs generate ensembles and analyse how any two characters clash or align. Export .json round-trips everything — traits, pins, sliders, pressure sheet, constraints, counts and toggles — so re-importing and pressing Generate reproduces the same character, not just the same sheet.

Reading the sheet. Coherence is how much the picks reinforce each other versus chance — the lift over baseline is the real number. Uncommon combinations are flagged tensions, not errors; they're often the most interesting thing on the sheet. The Use: line under each trait is a frequency budget — how often the trait should actually appear on the page before it tips into caricature.

1 · Who first, then how. Generation resolves the deep profile sections first — Motivation & Wound, Stress Response, Social Role, Values, Attachment, Humor, Vices — and only then draws the voice (vocabulary, grammar, mannerisms). Resolved deep facts feed forward: land on "Freeze" for stress and Disfluencies & Flow grammar gets likelier; land on "Skeptic" and precision vocabulary rises. Cause runs from character to voice, the way it does in actual people.

2 · The signal system. Sixteen signals — thirteen personality axes plus the three voice sliders — each carry a hand-built table of category affinities in three strengths (strong / moderate / weak). Every category draw sums all active signals into a weight map; Boost strength scales how hard that map bends the dice, with 0 = pure uniform random. Nothing is ever forced: a strongly-driven category is likelier, never guaranteed, which is why coherence is reported against a chance baseline instead of pretending determinism.

2b · What the wound pulls. The sliders are not the only signal. Motivation & Wound draws before anything else on the sheet, and what it draws steers what follows: a betrayal pulls Attachment toward Avoidant and Values toward Loyalty-Bound, a fear of abandonment pulls Attachment toward Anxious and Social Role toward Connector. It is keyed on the drawn trait's own language rather than on a category, because this section draws from every one of its categories on every sheet and so never resolves to a single one. Like every other cross-link it is scaled to nudge rather than decide — the whole section is normalised to one strong link's worth of pull, however many of its facets circle the same theme.

3 · Continuous intensity. Every trait occupies a fixed, continuous position on a 1–5 intensity scale (its declared level plus a stable per-trait offset, so twelve "intensity 3" traits are twelve distinct points, not one bucket). A slider's magnitude maps to a continuous target on the same scale; only traits inside a window around that target are eligible, and proximity to the target weights the draw. This is why −35 and −45 give measurably different results, and why each card shows an active range — the exact slider span where that trait can ever appear. Slider precision sets the window width: tight = sliders dictate, loose = sliders nudge.

4 · Trait-level polarity. Beyond categories, most traits carry their own small psychology vector ("this trait leans +honesty, −warmth"). At pick time that vector is scored against your current combined slider posture: agreeing traits get boosted, conflicting ones suppressed, scaled by Boost strength. So two characters can resolve the same category — say, "Core Want" — and still draw systematically different traits from it depending on posture. The why? panel reports this whenever it applied.

5 · Order of authority. When systems disagree: lock beats everything; pin beats sliders for that slot's intensity; archetype blends with (never replaces) your sliders; depth-first derives sliders but leaves any axis its resolved categories don't speak to untouched. Reroll always honours the slot's own current target, and remembers what you've rejected. Every one of these decisions is inspectable per-card via why?.

6 · Keeping it from going stale. Three controls exist specifically to fight sameness. Avoid recent traits softly penalises anything drawn in your last few characters. Divergence inverts the correlation matrix on a share of category draws, so the system stops optimising toward the most typical member of each cluster — coherence becomes a dial you set rather than a score you're graded on. The one thing that doesn't fit forces a single slot from the far tail of a random category with your slider posture switched off, because real people have exactly one trait that doesn't follow from the others. The sheet also reports distinctiveness against everything you've generated this session, which is a different question from the novelty note (that one only compares you to your last character).

Seed: —
Same seed + same settings = the exact same character, pressure sheet included, shareable as text. Every generation shows the seed it used — paste that seed back in to replay it, including the one the app generates for you when this box is blank. Every build draws against an empty session history, so the seed alone decides the character and it comes out the same in any session. A blank box is exploration: it draws three candidates and keeps the one least like your recent characters and your project archive, so Avoid recent traits steers which candidate is kept rather than the draw itself. The seed printed is the winner's, so pasting it back gives you that character. One deliberate exception either way: rerolls are un-seeded, because a reroll is you overriding the dice.

Rules — what must or must not appear

Constraints — always / never
Bans are hard filters — banned content can never appear, even where sliders point straight at it. "Always include" traits arrive locked on every generation; "at least one" only spends a slot when the build didn't already land in that category. If a trait is both banned and required, required wins: an explicit always is the stronger statement.

Budgets — rarity caps & intensity ceilings
Constraints say what can appear. Budgets say how much. Both run after the draw, so nothing here distorts the weighting or the why? explanations — a budget adjusts the result and tells you it did. Slots you locked, pinned or required are never modified, but they still spend the budget, so you can see when you've spent it yourself.

Divergence is the counterweight to all of this: on a share of category draws proportional to the setting, the correlation matrix's pull is deliberately inverted, so the system stops walking toward the most typical member of each cluster. Coherence is then something you choose rather than something you are graded on. Higher boost strength = current sliders bias category selection more strongly toward "paired" categories, AND bias which individual trait gets drawn within a category toward ones whose own psychology agrees with your current slider posture (a trait's "why?" panel shows this when it applies). 0 = pure uniform random. Verbosity, Register, and Composure now feed the same system personality sliders do — they influence Motivation, Stress Response, Social Role, Values, Attachment, Humor, and Vices too, not just tone. Slider precision controls how narrow the intensity window is around each slider's target — narrower means -35 and -45 draw from more clearly different traits. Profile weight sets how intensely Motivation/Values/Role/etc. traits read, independent of the voice sliders.

Library — packs and presets

Content packs
The bank ships as packs. Turning one off removes its traits from every draw — useful for keeping a setting clean, or for seeing what a pack actually contributes. The choice travels with exports and saved settings. node tools/studio.js packs prints the same list.

Custom archetype builder
Set the sliders above however you like, then save them as a reusable archetype. The constraints, counts and section toggles in force are saved alongside — selecting the archetype still just blends its posture with yours, and "Restore full setup" is the explicit way to get the rest back. The library exports as a file, so a set of house archetypes can be shared or moved between machines.

What the markings on each trait mean
●●●○○ dots — intensity, 1–5. How strongly the trait shows. Low-intensity traits are background texture; high-intensity ones dominate a scene.
common / uncommon / distinctive / signature — how sharply the trait defines a voice. Common is ordinary human behaviour: texture, not identity. Uncommon is a shade more particular than most people are. Distinctive is specific enough to notice and not enough to carry a character on its own. Signature defines the voice, and is genuinely rare — about a sixth of the bank rather than the two thirds the old two-class labelling produced, because when most entries are billed as defining, none of them are. Rarity is not loudness: it answers how many people are like this, and intensity separately answers how loudly it shows, so a quiet signature trait — an ordinary person with one startling verbal habit — is a thing the bank can express. The rarity preference weights each tier by its own size within the pool being drawn from, so "balanced" means balanced regardless of how a particular category happens to be composed.
keep — hold this exact trait so rebuilding or tossing elsewhere on the sheet won't replace it.
pin — different from lock: keeps this slot's intensity target fixed (shown as a number, e.g. "pinned 3.4") so future generations and rerolls keep drawing from around that level even as sliders move elsewhere — but the actual trait can still vary. Use +/− to nudge the pinned level. Lock overrides pin if both are set.
active range — the exact span of slider magnitude this specific trait can be drawn at. Outside that window it's never eligible, however the dice fall. The tick mark shows the slot's current target inside that window.
toss — redraw just this slot. It won't hand you back a trait you already rejected here.
presentation lock — three categories (Confidence, Emotional Capacity, Intelligence) cover two genuinely different presentations of the same pole — insecure vs. grandiose, guarded vs. shallow, instinctive vs. unanalytical. Each character commits to one at generation, so a sheet never mixes self-loathing with preening. The why? panel names which one is locked, and it travels with the character in exports and seeds.
outlier — the sheet's one deliberate misfit, present only when "the one thing that doesn't fit" is switched on. Drawn from the far tail of a randomly chosen category with your slider posture ignored for that single draw, because everything else in the engine pulls toward consistency and real people have exactly one trait that doesn't follow from the others.
why? — shows which slider or resolved trait actually caused this pick, or tells you it was an unweighted draw. When the trait itself carries a psychological lean, it also shows whether that lean currently agrees or conflicts with your sliders, and by how much.
↺ was: "…" — this slot changed on the last reroll; shows what it replaced.
Coherence — share of picks reinforced by another trait, shown against the chance baseline for your current settings. The lift over baseline is the meaningful figure.
Uncommon combinations — flagged pairings that are unusual but perfectly playable. These are notes, not errors, and nothing is blocked.
